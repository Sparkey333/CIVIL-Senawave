### 2. Certification versus seal in this case

The certification is Brandon's written statement: what he reviewed, on what basis, what he concludes, and what he excludes. The seal is the Utah statutory mark of responsibility under 58-22-602 (final plans, specifications and reports bear the seal when submitted to a client or public authority) [2nd-hand]. UBC wants both: p37 says "UBC accepts stamped PDF files" and the UBC draft subgrant agreement has both a "stamped or sealed by a Professional Engineer" clause for project plans (section 3(a)(3), the Indeed route-plan-set scope) and a separate PE certification of network design, cost and build-out timeline (section 6) [2nd-hand]. These are two deliverables; the BEAD certification is the second one and is outside the posting Brandon was hired under.

What the seal attests to is governed by 58-22-603: a licensee may seal work he prepared, work prepared under his supervision, or work of others where he "clearly identifies the portion" he is responsible for, "performs a thorough review" of it, and makes any necessary corrections before it is represented as final [2nd-hand]. Brandon is on the third prong. Three consequences follow and every later section implements them: (a) the certification must name the portion reviewed and the method (the Basis of Review schedule); (b) anything not thoroughly reviewed is excluded on the sealed page (the Exclusions schedule); (c) "thorough review" must be provable later, which is what the review log, the native files and his own check sheets are for. Without those the seal reads as plan-stamping, the conduct 58-22-603 and the NSPE responsible-charge position exist to prevent.

Discipline: Brandon's Utah license is civil. NTIA names no discipline (the PE may be licensed in any of the 56 eligible entities) [2nd-hand] and the Utah seal carries no discipline. The exposure is competence, not licensure. Routing, conduit, aerial, quantities, unit-cost arithmetic and scheduling are civil work. The optical layer is arithmetic against a datasheet, which he already reproduced in round 1 (Cedar Fort 27.7, Willard 26.1, Sundance 24.1, Saratoga Springs 24.1 dB). The three plans differ on how far to go: the liability plan wants a supporting statement from Senawave's network engineer or a telecom PE; the compliance plan wants personal verification if every area clears 3 dB and a specialist memo otherwise; the practical plan wants his own worksheet with an optional peer check at Senawave's cost. Resolution: personal verification worksheets are the baseline and sufficient when every area shows 3 dB or more margin against the actual Ubiquiti UACC-UF-OM-XGS optic class; a short signed peer check by an OSP/PON engineer, paid by Senawave as an allowance in the task order, is required if any area still sits below 3 dB after the rebuild (Cedar Fort at 2.3 dB today) or if the Sundance 1x32 at HH-142 is not resolved. Latency and peak capacity are operational measurements by Senawave; the certification covers the design basis only and says so.

Role label: p37 calls Brandon "engineering and stamping consultant, engaged per project". Replace it everywhere with "Engineer of Record (independent review and certification)". The word "stamping" invites exactly the accusation 58-22-603 addresses.

### 5. Sealing mechanics for a PDF report in Utah

Rule, to be verified on the primary text before issue: R156-22-601 requires the seal to carry the licensee's name, license number, "State of Utah" and "Professional Engineer" [2nd-hand, Cornell LII snippet]; round-1 notes and the tracker page add circular, 1.5 inch minimum, signature and date across the face, cover or title sheet at minimum, copies acceptable if seal, signature and date remain recognizable, electronic seals acceptable with adequate security, final documents only [2nd-hand; the 1.5 inch figure was found only on a stamp-vendor page this round]. 58-22-602: final reports submitted to a client or public authority bear the seal [2nd-hand].

Steps, one PDF per project:
1. Receive the final PDF (Rev C or later) and confirm its SHA-256 against Jesse's email. Open it once. Do not edit content; anything wrong goes back as a revision, never fixed in Acrobat.
2. Confirm the cover reads "Rev [1], Issued for Certification, [date]", carries the project ID, the page-count statement ("This document consists of [N] numbered pages and Appendix A of [M] pages; only a complete copy bearing the engineer's seal, signature and date on this cover and on page [x] is a sealed document"), a revision table, and a 2 x 2 inch clear box; confirm the certification page has the same box. No "Draft", "OPEN", "Facts to gather", "remove before sending", "TBD" or "under negotiation" anywhere in the file.
3. Seal artwork: 1.75 inch PNG with transparent background (clears the 1.5 inch minimum after any scaling), from a Utah stamp vendor or scanned from the wet stamp. Keep the file off shared drives; never hand it to Jesse "to drop in".
4. In Acrobat (or Bluebeam): place the seal as a custom stamp in the box on the cover and the certification page; check the size with the measure tool.
5. Sign across the face (stylus or signature image) and type the date across the seal below the signature. Same date in both places and on the digital signature.
6. Flatten stamps and annotations so the seal becomes page content.
7. Certify the PDF with a certificate-based digital ID from an AATL-recognised CA (IdenTrust, GlobalSign, Sectigo document-signing certificates run roughly $100 to $300 a year), visible signature next to the seal on the cover, permitted changes "No changes allowed". "Adequate security" is not defined in the rule; a CA-issued certificate is the easy answer if DOPL or UBC asks. Do not use a self-signed ID.
8. Save as `Senawave_BEAD_VAI38_Network_Design_Certification_Rev1_SEALED_[date].pdf` (and VAI39). Compute and record the SHA-256 in Schedule A, the review log and the transmittal. Email the file to David Bradshaw with the transmittal; do not deliver through a shared Drive folder where it can be renamed or re-saved.
9. The owner's statement is signed by David Bradshaw on Senawave's own page the same day; it is not under the seal.
10. Archive the sealed PDF, the unsealed PDF, the natives, the review log, the check sheets, the datasheet, the engagement letter and the E&O confirmation in one read-only folder Brandon controls, for ten years (covers Utah's construction repose window and the federal record period; the plans cited 7, 10 and 3-years-from-final-report; ten is the safe choice).

Never: seal a draft; seal pages not reviewed; seal a combined two-project statement; sign a date earlier than the signing day; re-save a sealed PDF (it breaks the certificate).

### 8. Re-issue rules

| Change after sealing | Re-issue? | How |
|---|---|---|
| The defined start date (assumed execution date) slides; relative schedule unchanged; every finish still inside the period of performance and the four-year test | No | One-paragraph signed letter to Dave noting the new assumed date and that the certification stands; log entry. |
