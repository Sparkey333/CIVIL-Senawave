01

Aerial on existing, proposed, or both?

Neither · survey linework only

Keep aerial photography off the Existing Conditions, Demolition, Site, Grading, Utility, and Plan-and-Profile sheets. Put it on the cover vicinity inset and, if a board or neighbor needs context, one standalone exhibit sheet.

The construction sheets carry the sealed survey. A photo under that linework introduces a second, unrectified version of "existing" that can disagree with the boundary and topo by several feet, dies on half-size prints, muddies grayscale plots, and goes stale. Every agency standard that addresses the question lands in the same place.

SheetAerial?Treatment
Cover · vicinity map insetOptionalSmall inset, grayscale OK, site outlined, north arrow
Context / Overall Site ExhibitOptionalGrayscale or faded, footprint overlaid, date and source note, "FOR REFERENCE ONLY · NOT A SURVEY"
Existing Conditions / TopoNoScreened survey linework, sealed by the surveyor
DemolitionNoScreened existing, hatched removals
Site · Grading · Utility · ESCNoScreened existing, bold proposed
Plan and profileNoSame; clutter kills the profile
Offsite drainage area mapSometimesGrayscale aerial, USGS or LiDAR base is common where the watershed exceeds the survey

If an aerial does go on an exhibit: xref it, send to back with DRAWORDER, fade it 50 to 70 percent through the plot style or image transparency, keep survey linework on top, and note the capture date and source.

02

Where the rule comes from

Caltrans · CADD Users Manual ch. 4

Raster imagery such as aerial photos is not to be used as background for plan sheets such as layouts, drainage, utilities, or pavement delineation. Aerials belong on dedicated Aerial Identification sheets or a detail sheet that must emphasize one unique location, only where engineering need justifies it.

The Plans Preparation Manual adds that photos clutter plan sheets and suit preliminary plans better than contract plans, and that any aerial shown must carry its date and source.

DoD · Civil/Site Development ch. 3

An aerial photo may stand in for survey data only at the conceptual level, when survey is not yet available, with the photo date noted. Construction-level drawings are survey based.

Port of Portland · CAD Standards

Aerial images are not accepted for use as a site plan.

USACE · Mississippi Valley Division CAD Supplement

Existing or unchanged features are drawn screened (half-tone) on plan, profile, and section sheets. Any aerials that appear are plotted in grayscale; sheet files plot grayscale only.

City of Mesa · Engineering CAD Standards

The aerial is a separate reference file, scaled to the project horizontal datum with the grid-to-ground factor applied. That is the right CAD setup: a toggleable reference, never part of the sheet's base linework.

Arlington County VA · Site Plan Specifications

The one jurisdiction found that asks for an aerial: a current orthophoto of the site and surrounding blocks as a context exhibit with the site plan application. An entitlement exhibit, not a construction sheet.

Philadelphia · ePlan Submission Standards

Any reference drawing or image must be labeled or watermarked as reference only. Civil existing and proposed sheets carry the C prefix.

EBMUD · WMATA · San Diego · Arlington CADD

Site plans show existing features with screened lines and only new work in bold. Existing is dashed, proposed solid. Screening is produced by the plot style table, not by drawing gray geometry.

03

A simple, complete sheet index

Consensus of the Ann Arbor, Denton, Frisco, Lancaster, Wake Forest, Tolleson, Rolesville, Selma, McKinney, and Gilroy checklists and the Bohler-pattern permit sets below. Toggle the project conditions to see which sheets a typical small commercial or multifamily site needs. Numbering follows National CAD Standard sheet types (C-0xx general, C-1xx plans, C-2xx profiles, C-5xx details); the C-101 / C-201 / C-301 firm pattern is equally accepted.

C-001
Core

Cover Sheet

  • Project name, address, owner, engineer, surveyor
  • Vicinity map (aerial inset acceptable here)
  • Sheet index in set order, count matches the set
  • Site data table: zoning, parcel, area, impervious, parking
  • Benchmark and datum statement, utility contacts or 811 note
  • Engineer's seal, agency approval block and signature lines
C-002
Core

General Notes and Legend

  • General, grading, utility, paving, ESC notes
  • Legend of existing (screened) vs proposed (bold) symbols and linetypes
  • Abbreviations; jurisdiction's standard notes verbatim where required
C-100
Core

Existing Conditions / Topographic Survey

  • Boundary with bearings and distances, easements, adjoiners, benchmarks
  • Existing contours and spot grades, trees, structures, pavement
  • All existing utilities with rim and invert elevations
  • Floodplain and wetland lines; topo to ROW centerline and 50 to 100 ft beyond
Keep off: any proposed work. Usually the surveyor's sheet and seal.
C-101
When removing structures

Demolition Plan

  • Items to be removed, hatched or heavy dashed, with REMOVE callouts
  • Items to remain and protect; tree protection
  • Limits of disturbance; utility abandonment and cap notes
Tip: combine with C-100 on small sites only if it stays legible.
C-200
Core

Site Plan (Layout and Dimensioning)

  • Building footprint and FFE; curb and pavement with dimensions and radii
  • Parking counts and ADA; sidewalks; setbacks and easements
  • Striping, signage, dumpster, light pole locations if civil
  • Horizontal control or coordinate table
Keep off: contours and utilities except where they dictate layout.
C-300
Core

Grading and Drainage Plan

  • Existing contours screened, proposed contours bold
  • Spot grades at curbs, corners, ADA routes, doors, high and low points
  • Storm structures and pipes with size, slope, material; drainage arrows
  • Retaining walls with TW/BW; ponds or BMPs with contours and outlet
Keep off: underground dry utilities (Ann Arbor checklist).
C-301
When BMPs are required

Stormwater Management Plan and Details

  • Pond or underground detention plan and sections
  • Outlet control structure detail
  • Drainage area map (grayscale aerial or LiDAR base acceptable offsite)
C-400
Core

Utility Plan

  • Water: size, fittings, valves, hydrants, meter, backflow
  • Sanitary: manholes, cleanouts, inverts, slope
  • Storm shown lightly for conflicts; fire line; dry conduits by reference
  • Crossings and separations called out
C-401+
When mains are public or long

Plan and Profile Sheets

  • One per utility run needing stationing: sanitary, storm, water
  • Plan over profile on the same sheet, see section 04
C-500
Core

Erosion and Sediment Control Plan

  • Perimeter controls, inlet protection, stabilized construction entrance
  • Concrete washout, stockpile, phasing notes
  • ESC details or standard reference; SWPPP data as required
C-600
When the site plan is crowded

Paving, Signing and Striping

  • Pavement sections by area, joint layout if concrete
  • Signs and striping schedule
C-700
Core

Site Details

  • Curbs, pavement sections, sidewalk and ADA ramps
  • Dumpster pad, signs, bollards
C-701
Core

Utility and Storm Details

  • Manholes, inlets, trench, thrust blocks, hydrants, cleanouts
  • Cite agency standard detail numbers instead of redrawing where allowed
L / E
Separate disciplines

Landscape · Site Lighting

  • L-100 Landscape Plan, L-500 Details (usually a zoning requirement)
  • E-100 Site Lighting and Photometrics if required

Rules that recur across the checklists

  • One purpose per sheet. When a layout gets crowded, move work to another sheet instead of cramming (Caltrans, Rolesville, Ann Arbor).
  • Every sheet: north arrow, bar scale, written scale, title block with engineer name, address, license, seal, sheet title and number, revision block.
  • Benchmark on grading, utility, and profile sheets; horizontal control ties where the city has monuments (McKinney).
  • Existing screened, proposed bold. Set it in the CTB/STB, never by drawing gray lines.
  • Index count equals sheet count. Reviewers check it (Rolesville, Gilroy).
  • Approval sets are signed and sealed on every sheet. Preliminary sets read "Preliminary, Not for Construction" (Walpole, Bennett).
04

Plan and profile conventions

LayoutPlan view above, profile directly below, on the same sheet, stationed to match (Ann Arbor, Franklin WI).
Scales1"=40' horizontal with 1"=4' vertical is the common pairing; 1"=20' with 1"=2' where congested (Ann Arbor, Madera, Denton, Salisbury, Palm Springs). Sutter County allows 20/40/50 H with 2/4/5 V. Spartanburg caps sewer at 1"=100' by 1"=10'. Use only the scale the sheet is built for.
Grade linesExisting grade dashed and screened, proposed finish grade solid, both labeled along the run.
StructuresStation, rim or top, invert in and out at every manhole and inlet. Structure numbers match plan to profile.
Pipe labelsLength, size, material, class, slope, in plan or profile but consistent across the set.
CrossingsExisting and proposed utilities that cross the run are shown and dimensioned for vertical clearance (Denton, Scottsdale).
StationingEvery 50 or 100 ft; road profiles with centerline elevations at 50 ft and vertical curve elevations at 25 ft (Elk Grove).
HGLHydraulic grade line on storm profiles where the agency asks; 100-year depth and capacity under roadways (Scottsdale).
Separate profilesOne profile per utility unless in a common trench (Clinton Township MI).
DatumBenchmark description and vertical datum on every profile sheet.
